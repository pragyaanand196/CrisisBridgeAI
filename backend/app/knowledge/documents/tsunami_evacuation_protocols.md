# INCOIS & NDMA Tsunami Early Warning & Coastal Evacuation Protocol (2024)
**Document ID:** INCOIS-NDMA-TSU-2024-V2
**Source Agency:** Indian National Centre for Ocean Information Services (INCOIS) / NDMA
**Disaster Category:** Tsunami / Seismic Ocean Wave / Coastal Inundation
**Applicable Environment:** Coastal boundaries, beach frontage, maritime estuaries, low-lying islands
**Last Updated:** 2024-06-01

---

### Section 1: Natural Warning Signs & Immediate Coastal Evacuation
1. **Natural Warning Signs:** A severe earthquake near the coast that lasts longer than 20 seconds, sudden unusual roaring ocean sound like a freight train, or rapid dramatic receding of coastal sea waters exposing the seabed are immediate natural tsunami warnings.
2. **Do Not Wait for Official Sirens:** If you observe natural warning signs, evacuate inland and to high ground immediately on foot. Do not wait for official siren confirmations.
3. **Move to Elevated Ground:** Evacuate to an elevation of at least **15 meters (50 feet)** above sea level, or move at least **2 to 3 kilometers** inland away from beaches and coastal estuaries. If evacuation inland is blocked, take vertical shelter on the 3rd floor or higher of a reinforced concrete multi-story building.

---

### Section 2: Wave Succession & Vessel Safety at Sea
1. **Multiple Waves Danger:** A tsunami is a series of multiple powerful waves occurring minutes to hours apart; the first wave is rarely the largest. Stay on high ground until official "All Clear" bulletins are issued by disaster authorities.
2. **Boats and Ships:** Do not attempt to return to port if already deep at sea (depth > 100 meters), as tsunamis are barely perceptible in deep ocean water but build immense destructive height in shallow coastal waters.
3. **Emergency Numbers:** INCOIS Coastal Alert Desk **1800-425-5225**, Coast Guard **1554**, National Emergency **112**.
