# NDMA & IMD National Heatwave Action Plan & Health Protection Protocol (2024)
**Document ID:** NDMA-HW-2024-V4
**Source Agency:** National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) / India Meteorological Department (IMD) / WHO
**Disaster Category:** Heatwave / Extreme Temperature / Heatstroke Hazard
**Applicable Environment:** Urban heat islands, arid plains, outdoor agricultural & construction zones
**Last Updated:** 2024-04-30

---

### Section 1: Peak Sun Exposure Avoidance & Outdoor Safety
1. **Restrict Afternoon Exposure:** Avoid strenuous outdoor physical exertion, direct sun exposure, and travel between **11:00 AM and 4:00 PM** when solar irradiance and wet-bulb temperatures are highest.
2. **Protective Clothing:** Wear lightweight, light-colored, loose-fitting breathable cotton clothing. Cover head and face with a wide-brimmed hat, cloth, or umbrella when moving outdoors.
3. **Never Leave Persons or Pets in Parked Vehicles:** Internal vehicle temperatures can exceed fatal levels (50°C+) within 10 minutes even with cracked windows.

---

### Section 2: Hydration & Oral Rehydration Protocols
1. **Drink Ample Fluids:** Drink frequent sips of water even before feeling thirsty. Consume traditional electrolyte-rich hydrating drinks: Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS), homemade lemon water (shikanji), buttermilk (chaas), coconut water, and aam panna.
2. **Avoid Dehydrating Beverages:** Avoid high-caffeine energy drinks, strong coffee, alcohol, and excessive carbonated sodas which accelerate bodily dehydration.
3. **Cooling Techniques:** Use cool water sponging, damp towels on neck and forehead, and take cool baths to lower core body temperature.

---

### Section 3: Recognizing Heat Exhaustion & Heatstroke Emergency
1. **Heatstroke Symptoms:** High body temperature (104°F / 40°C+), hot red dry skin (no sweating), rapid pounding pulse, throbbing headache, dizziness, nausea, confusion, or loss of consciousness.
2. **Emergency First Aid:** Heatstroke is a medical emergency. Move patient immediately to a cool shaded area, loosen tight clothing, fan vigorously, apply ice packs to neck, armpits, and groin, and call **108** (Ambulance) / **112** immediately.
3. **Emergency Numbers:** National Ambulance **108**, Disaster Emergency **112** / **1077**.
